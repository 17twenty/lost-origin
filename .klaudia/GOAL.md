# Goal: Lost Origin — playable proof-of-concept level

## Objective
Build a small playable Godot 4.7 (`/Applications/Godot.app`) proof of concept based on `inspiration.mp4`, which shows a tropical island with mossy totem ruins mixed with a Halo-CE-style undersea facility, with a hint of Sonic Adventure. The player controls an original armoured-explorer character in third person with an over-the-shoulder camera. Movement should feel Halo-weighted: run, sprint and jump, with real momentum. The level is one cohesive scene. Outside there is a lagoon beach, totem pillars, a ruined aqueduct bridge, and a jungle waterfall grotto with a glowing teleporter pad. Inside there is an undersea facility: a hex-tiled corridor with aquarium windows and octagonal pools, an atrium with a central water column and a ring walkway, and a control room. You can walk seamlessly from the grotto into the facility. A teleporter pair also links the control room and the grotto pad in both directions. Geometry is a mix of our own procedural or Blender meshes and CC0 assets (Kenney, Quaternius, Poly Haven). The mood should match the video: saturated blue sky and water, bloom, glowing cyan trim, and caustic-like water.

**Round 2: fidelity pass.** Round 1 met every criterion above, but the spec was written from only 6 sampled frames. The full video (23 frames at 2 fps) shows a seventh composition that was missed (an indoor garden room) and lots of detail the level lacks: sea life, glass ceilings and walls onto the ocean, hanging vines, an aqueduct striding through the shallows, an open-air grotto, birds and sun glints. Round 2 brings the level in line with the video's 7 compositions: grotto, lagoon totems, garden room, aquarium corridor, aqueduct, atrium, and control room. All round-1 behaviour and tests must keep passing.

## Acceptance criteria
- [x] The Godot project is in the repo root (`project.godot`) and imports headless with no errors.

- [x] Third-person controller: WASD and mouse-look over-the-shoulder camera, sprint (Shift), and jump (Space) with acceleration and deceleration. The camera handles collisions, so it doesn't clip through walls in corridors.
- [x] Outdoor zones exist and have collision: beach/lagoon (shader water), totem pillars, aqueduct bridge you can walk across, and waterfall grotto with the teleporter pad.
- [x] Indoor zones exist and have collision: aquarium corridor (windows looking out on an underwater backdrop, plus octagonal pools), atrium (water column plus a ring walkway you can reach), and control room (consoles).
- [x] There is a seamless path from the grotto to the facility (doorway, then a ramp or lift) with no loading screen.
- [x] The teleporter pair (grotto pad ↔ control room pad) moves the player both ways, with a visual effect.
- [x] The look: WorldEnvironment with glow/bloom, a bright sky, fog or volumetrics, emissive cyan trim indoors, and an animated water shader.
- [x] CC0 assets are stored under `assets/third_party/`, with `CREDITS.md` listing each source URL and its licence.
- [x] Scenes are built by re-runnable generator scripts in `tools/`, not by hand-splicing `.tscn` files.
- [x] Automated tests in `tests/` pass headless. They cover: the player spawns on the ground, scripted input moves the player from the beach across the bridge into the grotto, the player walks down into the atrium through the seamless path, both teleporter directions work, and the player can't fall out of the world in any zone.
- [x] `tools/capture_screenshot.gd` renders one in-game screenshot per zone (at least 6) to `art/screens/`. I'll review them against the video frames.

- [ ] **Sea life:** animated fish schools are visible through the corridor windows, in the atrium water column and outside the control room (fish meshes or impostors in a `fish` group; a test confirms there are at least 30 fish and that their positions change over time). Kelp has a swaying vertex shader, and coral/rock clusters sit on the sea floor of the ocean backdrops.
- [ ] **Glass onto the ocean:** the atrium gets a glass ceiling with water and light rays visible above, plus round or octagonal porthole windows in its walls. The control room's far wall becomes a full-width glass panorama onto the ocean, with the consoles lined up facing it as in the video. The glass collides (test), and all backdrops share one ocean look (gradient, caustics, rays, haze).
- [ ] **Hanging moss and vines** on the totems, aqueduct piers and deck edge, and grotto walls: our own Blender vine/moss-strand meshes with a gentle wind-sway shader. A test confirms there are at least 40 vine instances.
- [ ] **Aqueduct like the video:** a long row of at least 8 mossy piers standing in shallow turquoise water along the beach, deck still walkable as part of the route (route tests updated; the chasm can become a shallow inlet). Piers have collision.
- [ ] **Open-air grotto like the video:** the main grotto basin is open to the sky (no solid roof over the pool and pad), ringed by cliff walls with dense jungle and hanging vines, with light shafts streaming in. The waterfall pours from the cliff lip into the pool. The pad gets steps and a raised ring like the video. The seamless door into the facility still works.
- [ ] **Lagoon and sky details:** the water shader adds sun-glint sparkles and caustic shimmer in the shallows, and at least 5 animated birds circle in the sky.
- [ ] **Secret switch and reveal:** a wall lever or switch in the control room shows an on-screen prompt ("E — Pull") when the player is within about 2 m and facing it. Pressing **E** plays the lever animation, then a short cutscene of 3–5 s: the camera cuts or blends to a view of a sealed door elsewhere in the facility and shows it opening (panels slide or retract, lights flicker on, sparks or dust, a light flood from inside). The camera then blends back to the player's over-the-shoulder camera and control resumes. Player input is ignored during the cutscene. The door's collision is removed once it's open, and the switch can't be re-triggered. Behind the door is a **small secret room** that isn't on the main route: a Forerunner relic chamber with a glowing artefact on a pedestal, cyan light and a window or skylight onto the ocean. Tests cover: E out of range does nothing; E in range starts the cutscene (cutscene camera current, movement input ignored); the door opens; the camera returns to the player within 6 s; the player can walk into the secret room; and a second E press does nothing. `Shot_Secret` frames the room.
- [ ] **Performance:** `tools/benchmark.gd` (non-headless) visits every `Shot_Ref_*` and reports an average FPS of at least 50 at 1280×720 on this Mac, printed as `BENCH avg_fps=<n>`.
- [ ] **Fidelity review:** all 7 `art/compare/*.png` pairs have been reviewed, with a short per-composition note in `art/compare/NOTES.md` saying what matches and any deliberate differences. Every round-1 and round-2 test passes headless.

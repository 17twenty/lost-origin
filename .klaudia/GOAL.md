# Goal: Lost Origin — playable proof-of-concept level

## Objective
Build a small playable Godot 4.7 (`/Applications/Godot.app`) proof of concept based on `inspiration.mp4`, which shows a tropical island with mossy totem ruins mixed with a Halo-CE-style undersea facility, with a hint of Sonic Adventure. The player controls an original armoured-explorer character in third person with an over-the-shoulder camera. Movement should feel Halo-weighted: run, sprint and jump, with real momentum. The level is one cohesive scene. Outside there is a lagoon beach, totem pillars, a ruined aqueduct bridge, and a jungle waterfall grotto with a glowing teleporter pad. Inside there is an undersea facility: a hex-tiled corridor with aquarium windows and octagonal pools, an atrium with a central water column and a ring walkway, and a control room. You can walk seamlessly from the grotto into the facility. A teleporter pair also links the control room and the grotto pad in both directions. Geometry is a mix of our own procedural or Blender meshes and CC0 assets (Kenney, Quaternius, Poly Haven). The mood should match the video: saturated blue sky and water, bloom, glowing cyan trim, and caustic-like water.

**Round 2: fidelity pass.** Round 1 met every criterion above, but the spec was written from only 6 sampled frames. The full video (23 frames at 2 fps) shows a seventh composition that was missed (an indoor garden room) and lots of detail the level lacks: sea life, glass ceilings and walls onto the ocean, hanging vines, an aqueduct striding through the shallows, an open-air grotto, birds and sun glints. **Round 2 also brings a new hero and a full asset pass.** The player becomes **the Wicker Man**: a towering protector woven from rattan and straw, *summoned* to the island by its ancient totems as its last (and lost) guardian, who now has to discover what "high-tech" infestation has taken root beneath it. At game start he **weaves himself into existence** (strands spiralling in and knitting together), then the player takes control. The design follows the user's turnaround (`wicker-inspo/turn-around-final.png`); the film stills in `wicker-inspo/` are weave and material reference only, and **our character's face is original, not any actor's likeness**. Every asset also gets a fidelity pass so it better approximates the source video. Round 2 also adds a **start page** and ends with the game **exported for the web and live at `https://lost-origin.curiola.com`**, served by the local Caddy (`~/Sites/Caddyfile`, behind Cloudflare). Round 2 brings the level in line with the video's 7 compositions: grotto, lagoon totems, garden room, aquarium corridor, aqueduct, atrium, and control room. All round-1 behaviour and tests must keep passing.

- [ ] **Foliage sway fix (user report: trees sway at the base rather than from the trunk — the whole tree moves):**
  - **Expected:** palms, the tree fern, the garden tree, ferns/monstera/grass, vines and straw must stay planted at the base/attachment, with motion only increasing toward frond and leaf tips and strand ends.
  - **Root cause (verified):** every Blender-built mesh reaches Godot with vertex colour R = 1.0 everywhere (`tree_palmTall.res`, `garden_tree_mesh.res`, `vine_a.res`: R 1.00..1.00), so the sway shaders (`foliage`, `vine`, `straw`) move every vertex, trunks and bases included. The bmesh "Col" layers aren't the active/render colour attribute when the glTF exporter runs with `export_vertex_color="ACTIVE"`.

    - Make the shaders robust: also gate sway by a height/attachment falloff, so a missing colour channel can never move a base.
  - **Tests:** an automated test asserts the sway-weight channel is ~0 at the base/root vertices and > 0.5 at the tips of each plant mesh. Visually check in a running game that trunks stay put.
